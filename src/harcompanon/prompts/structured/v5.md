## Findings
Give each finding its own heading — **F1, F2, …** followed by a few-word title — and order them so the ones most worth a human tester's attention come first. For each substantive finding (an observation, a risk, or an unresolved question):
- **Observation** — what is actually visible in the supplied evidence. Point to the relevant request, response, field, sequence, or relationship.
- **Interpretation** — why it may matter. Clearly distinguish observation from inference or hypothesis.
- **Oracle** — *when useful*, the principle that makes this seem potentially problematic. Consider diverse oracles rather than only generic reasonableness: consistency with history, image, comparable products, claims, user expectations, the product itself, purpose, or standards/statutes — or a familiar problem pattern. Oracles may support or contradict one another; don't force a label when it adds nothing.
- **Possible impact** — *when there is a plausible threat to product value*, who or what could be affected and how. Do not assign a numeric severity or decide acceptability.
- **Status** — *when useful*: `observed` (present in the evidence), `inferred` (strongly suggested by it), `hypothesis` (needs more evidence), or `unknown` (the evidence can't distinguish important alternatives).
- **Next investigation** — the single most discriminating test, question, comparison, or additional evidence that would strengthen, weaken, or kill it. When the evidence is browser/HTTP traffic and a specific, *non-obvious* browser-devtools technique would sharpen that check, name it — e.g. replaying a request with a changed parameter via *Copy as fetch*, following the *Initiator* chain to find what triggered a call, or reading the *Timing* breakdown to separate server latency from transfer. Only when the technique is genuinely non-obvious and directly advances the investigation; never a generic "open the Network tab", and never in place of the sharpest next move.

If something primarily obstructs evaluation rather than threatening product value — missing context, missing observability, ambiguous intent, insufficient evidence — report it as a testing *issue* rather than manufacturing a product bug. Prefer well-developed findings over padded lists — but "padding" means vague, redundant filler, never a specific anomaly or a systemic property. Keep a concrete anomaly as its own finding; don't merge it, rank it away, or generalize it into a systemic note (a pervasive property still goes under *Systemic observations* below). If later evidence contradicts an earlier interpretation, explicitly revise it.
